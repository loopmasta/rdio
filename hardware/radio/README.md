# rdio carrier board

A 57.4 × 25.5 mm two-layer board that joins an ESP32 38-pin DevKit (NodeMCU-32S style) and an SA818S / DRA818 radio module. KiCad 10; the schematic and the board are generated by the scripts in [tools/](tools/README.md), so they can be changed in code.

- The DevKit sits **on top of** the thin carrier board, component side up. Its own header pins are soldered into plated holes in the carrier from the bottom side (no sockets).
- The radio module sits on the **bottom** side, between the two pin rows. It is mounted turned by 180° so that its antenna pad faces the SMA connector.
- The SMA edge connector (KooingTech SMA-KE, female, for 1.6 mm boards) is at the end where the DevKit's Wi-Fi antenna is, opposite the DevKit's USB port. The silkscreen says `ANT end (SMA side)` and `USB end (DevKit above)`.
- The small parts are on the top side, in the 7–8 mm gap between the carrier and the DevKit.

## Connections

| Module (SA818S) | Signal | ESP32 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 RXD | UART to the module | GPIO17 (UART2 TX) | 9600 baud, AT commands |
| 17 TXD | UART from the module | GPIO16 (UART2 RX) | connected directly, no series resistor |
| 5 PTT | transmit request | GPIO21 | low = transmit; **10 k pull-up to 3.3 V**, so a floating, crashed or resetting ESP32 means receive |
| 6 PD | power down | none | tied to 3.3 V: the module is always on |
| 7 H/L | power level | GPIO19 | open drain, low = low power (0.5 W), released = high power (1 W). **Never drive it high, never tie it to a supply.** |
| 1 SQ | squelch / carrier detect | GPIO22 | input, connected directly |
| 3 AF_OUT | receive audio | GPIO34 (ADC) | 1 µF coupling capacitor into a 100 k / 100 k divider (1.65 V bias) |
| 18 MIC_IN | transmit audio | GPIO25 (DAC) | 1 µF, then 100 k in series, 1 k to ground with 33 nF across it: about 1/100 attenuation, corner near 4.8 kHz |
| 12 ANT | antenna | SMA | 0.8 mm trace, drawn by hand |
| 8 VBAT, 9 / 10 GND | supply | | see Power |

GPIO26 and the other pins of the DevKit are not used.

## Power

- The module's VBAT comes from the DevKit's VIN pin (5 V from the DevKit's own USB-C) through R9, a 0 Ω link. The module accepts 3.3 to 5.5 V (the RF power is specified at 4.0 V).
- At the module: C6 220 µF (low ESR, at least 10 V, 6.3 × 5.8 mm) and C7 100 nF. On the 3.3 V side: C2 100 µF and C3 100 nF.
- **FB1 (ferrite bead) and C8 (100 µF) are not fitted.** To try them, fit both and remove R9: the bead keeps current steps of the module (power level change, PTT) away from the ESP32's regulator. Build without them first and see whether Wi-Fi still drops when the power level switches.
- The module draws about 60 mA receiving, 650–750 mA transmitting at high power and 450–550 mA at low power (datasheet). All of it passes through the DevKit's USB path and its VIN pin. Use a 5 V source that can deliver at least 1 A (2 A is better), and check the DevKit's input protection. USB 500 mA from a computer can brown out during a high-power transmission.
- Do not power the module from the DevKit's 3.3 V pin. Check the DevKit's behaviour before connecting USB and an external 5 V supply at the same time.

## Building and first power-up

1. Solder the DevKit's header pins into the carrier from the bottom side, with the DevKit on top of the board.
2. Place the SA818S on the bottom side with its antenna pad towards the SMA connector. Pin numbers 1 to 18 are printed inside the module outline, and an open ring marks the ring on the top of the module: put the module's ring over the board's ring.
3. Solder the SMA connector by hand.
4. Connect a 50 Ω antenna or a dummy load **before the first transmission**, keep the antenna away from the DevKit's antenna and the audio wiring, and start the bench tests at low power. Transmitting needs the right amateur radio licence and an allowed frequency.

Gerber, drill, bill of materials and placement files for JLCPCB are in [../jlcpcb/radio/](../jlcpcb/README.md).

## Design status

The board is routed with Freerouting. KiCad's design rule check reports no unconnected items and no clearance errors or shorts. What remains:

- one error that is intentional: the antenna trace runs inside the keepout area `ant_corridor`, which keeps the autorouter away from it. Delete that area in KiCad to get a clean report.
- two very short dangling track ends (`/SA_TXD`, 0.3 mm, and `/HL`, 0.19 mm),
- silkscreen warnings (overlaps and clearance to copper and to the board edge),
- the schematic symbol has a pin 11 (NC) that the footprint does not draw as a pad, which is harmless,
- the schematic check warns that the `VBAT` power symbol and the `Board` symbol library are not found, because the folder has no symbol library table.

## Still to verify at the bench

1. The typical-application schematic on pages 4 and 5 of the datasheet (the coupling into MIC_IN and the bias on AF_OUT) against the values above.
2. The idle and active voltage levels of pin 1.
3. The AF_OUT amplitude (peak or peak to peak) at each module volume setting.
4. The MIC_IN impedance, then change R4 (1 k) so that a 1750 Hz tone gives the right deviation (about 3 kHz), checked on a receiver or an SDR.

## Known limitations

- The antenna on the SMA connector is at the same end as the DevKit's Wi-Fi antenna, so a whip on the connector sits a few centimetres from it. A short coax pigtail, or an antenna mounted away from the board, helps more than any copper.
- Two RF filters were left out on purpose: 47–100 pF across R4 (2.4 GHz pickup into MIC_IN) and small series resistors and capacitors on the control lines (433 MHz pickup). The circuit is as shown.
- The 3D models are simple placeholders, not accurate models.

More details (footprints, the SMA connector, the shielding by copper pours, and a 4-layer variant): [design-notes.md](design-notes.md).
